Divining Principles from Scripture
Principles for Era of Transformation
• Core Premise: Holy scriptures exist to divine enduring principles to navigate new eras of transformation and spiritual renewal (rather than a literal second coming).

• Jesus & Sabbath Law: Reinterpreted Rabbinic and Pharisaic traditions around the Ten Commandments (e.g., plucking grain on the Sabbath in Mark 2:23–28: "The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath").

• Muhammad & Semitic Traditions: Reshaped ancient Semitic and Arabian practices into a unified monotheistic framework.

• Quranic Foundation: Surah Ar-Ra'd (13:38) — "For every age there is a revelation."
---

Revised Statements

Option 1: Theological & Formal

"These holy scriptures were given to us so that we might examine them and divine their enduring principles for a new coming—not a literal return, but a new era of spiritual transformation. We see this dynamic throughout history: Jesus radically reinterpreted the traditional Rabbinic understanding of the Ten Commandments, such as redefining the Sabbath when his disciples gathered grain; and Muhammad reshaped the ancient Semitic traditions of the Arabian peninsula. As the Quran beautifully affirms, 'For every age there is a revelation,' reminding us that divine principles must be divined anew to guide each changing period of human history."

Option 2: Direct & Conversational

"Holy scriptures exist for us to look beneath the surface and divine core principles for moments of major cultural shift. This isn't about a literal second coming, but a recurring wave of change. Look at how Jesus challenged the established Pharisaic view of the Sabbath by prioritizing human need over rigid ritual law, effectively changing how the Commandments were understood and applied. Similarly, Muhammad reshaped the ancient Semitic practices of his era. The Quran captures this exact concept by stating that every age is given its own revelation suited to its time."
